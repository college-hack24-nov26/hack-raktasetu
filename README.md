# RaktaSetu — System Requirements and Architecture Specification

**Version:** 1.0 — Initial design baseline  
**Status:** Proposed; requires workflow validation with participating blood banks  
**Implementation status:** Design only; no implementation authorised by this document  
**Initial geographic scope:** Maharashtra, India  
**Initial pilot recommendation:** One verified blood bank and a small number of its associated camps

---

## 1. Product vision

RaktaSetu is a blood supply chain traceability and availability management platform designed to track individual blood units from collection through testing, processing, storage, issue, and final recorded disposition.

The platform will connect donors, authorised collection teams, donation camps, blood banks, receiving facilities, and patients through a shared, auditable system.

Its primary objectives are:

1. Trace every collection unit and its derived components.
2. Maintain reliable inventory records and current availability.
3. Connect authorised donation camps with participating blood banks.
4. Give patients a clear way to find blood banks and contact them.
5. Retain donor discovery as a separate fallback when suitable blood-bank options are unavailable.
6. Provide real-time notifications and responsive, mobile-friendly workflows.
7. Preserve privacy, role separation, clinical accountability, and operational history.

### Core product principle

Every important inventory quantity must be explainable through underlying records. Every unit status change must have an authorised basis. Every recorded event must identify its source and preserve an auditable history.

## 2. Scope and boundaries

### Included in the intended full lifecycle

- Public blood-bank directory.
- Verified organisation onboarding.
- Donor registration and consent.
- Camp registration and blood-bank association.
- Donor attendance and donation-event records.
- Collection-unit identification.
- Camp reconciliation and physical handover.
- Blood-bank receipt and processing.
- Component-level traceability.
- Inventory and expiry management.
- Reservations, issue, transfers and returns where supported.
- Final recorded disposition.
- Patient-facing availability search.
- Real-time notifications.
- Audit, reconciliation and reporting.
- Receiving-facility integration.

### Explicit boundaries

RaktaSetu will not independently determine medical eligibility, authorise clinical release, replace required laboratory testing, or make transfusion decisions.

Those activities remain under qualified personnel and applicable procedures.

A software status such as `AVAILABLE` must represent a state established through the approved workflow—not an assertion generated by a public user or an unverified data import.

## 3. User roles and access model

A single person may have more than one role, but each action must be authorised in its actual organisational context.

| Role | Main responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Public visitor | Search the directory and view permitted public information |
| Patient/requester | Search availability, create requests and coordinate with authorised participants |
| Donor | Manage profile, consent, invitations and donation history |
| Camp organiser | Register camps, manage authorised participation and reconcile camp records |
| Collection staff | Record authorised collection events and related identifiers |
| Blood-bank administrator | Manage organisation settings, staff and approved workflows |
| Blood-bank inventory staff | Receive, locate, move and reconcile units |
| Authorised clinical staff | Record permitted screening, testing, processing and release decisions |
| Receiving-facility staff | Record authorised receipt, return and disposition events |
| Platform administrator | Manage platform access, organisation applications and technical operations |
| Auditor or compliance reviewer | Review authorised records and audit history |

### Permission requirements

- Each account belongs to an identified user.
- Organisation membership and role assignments are explicit.
- Organisation data is isolated by default.
- Clinical and personal information is restricted by purpose and role.
- Platform administrators do not automatically receive clinical decision-making authority.
- Sensitive actions require appropriate permission and audit records.
- Revoked accounts lose access without deleting historical events.
- Emergency or exceptional access, if required, must have a defined policy and audit trail.

## 4. Public directory versus organisation onboarding

These are separate workflows.

### Public directory import

RaktaSetu may import verified public directory information, such as:

- Official facility name.
- Public address.
- District and locality.
- Public contact information.
- Source reference and retrieval timestamp.

Imported information must retain its source and freshness metadata.

A directory listing does not establish that a facility has registered with RaktaSetu, authorised staff access, or supplied its own live inventory.

### Organisation onboarding

1. An authorised representative submits an application.
2. The platform records the application and supporting documents.
3. A designated reviewer checks the organisation's identity and applicable authorisation.
4. The application is approved, rejected or returned for correction.
5. An approved organisation activates its own administrator account.
6. Additional staff receive individual accounts and explicit permissions.

Each organisation must have a unique identifier. Shared passwords across blood banks are prohibited.

### Existing external availability

eRaktKosh availability may be displayed as externally reported information, with source and freshness clearly identified.

External snapshots must remain distinct from RaktaSetu's unit-level inventory. A public availability result does not establish that RaktaSetu knows the identity or complete lifecycle of each external unit.

## 5. End-to-end blood lifecycle

The lifecycle is modelled as related, auditable events rather than a single status field.

### Stage A — Camp approval

- Organiser creates a camp.
- Organiser selects a participating blood bank.
- Blood bank reviews the association request.
- Required operational permissions are established.
- Camp becomes eligible for the authorised workflow.

Platform approval does not replace statutory permissions or professional responsibilities.

### Stage B — Donor participation

- Donor registers or is found through an authorised lookup.
- Identity is confirmed using the approved procedure.
- Donor is associated with the camp.
- Authorised personnel perform the applicable screening.
- The outcome is recorded with restricted access.

Registration, attendance, screening and donation are separate events.

### Stage C — Collection

- An authorised collection event is recorded.
- The physical collection unit receives an identifier linked to the official identifier.
- Collection metadata is captured.
- The system records the responsible staff member and event timestamp.
- The donor's donation record is linked to the collection event.

The system must not assume that every donation produces an identical volume or number of transfusable components.

### Stage D — Camp reconciliation

- Collection records are checked.
- Recorded unit identifiers are reconciled.
- Duplicate and missing records are identified.
- The camp submits a versioned handover manifest.
- The receiving blood bank is notified.

Closing the camp does not automatically confirm receipt of its units.

### Stage E — Transport and receipt

- Dispatch and handover events are recorded where applicable.
- The receiving bank verifies expected unit identifiers.
- Each unit is accepted, held or recorded as discrepant.
- The physical receipt is reconciled with the submitted manifest.

An electronic submission is not proof of physical receipt.

### Stage F — Testing and processing

- Applicable testing and processing events are recorded.
- Results and references are access-controlled.
- Holds and failed checks prevent inappropriate release.
- Component creation links each resulting component to its source collection unit.
- Authorised personnel record release decisions.

### Stage G — Storage and inventory

- Released units are assigned to an appropriate inventory location.
- Component-specific expiry and status are maintained.
- Inventory summaries are calculated from the authoritative unit records.
- Transfers, reservations, issues, returns and adjustments are recorded as authorised events.

### Stage H — Issue and final disposition

- An authorised issue event records the unit and receiving facility.
- The receiving facility records receipt if integrated.
- Subsequent authorised movements or returns are recorded.
- Final disposition is recorded when supported by reliable evidence.

The system must distinguish issue from receipt, and receipt from transfusion. It must never invent an unobserved final outcome.

## 6. Unit identity and component traceability

The data model must distinguish three primary entities.

### Donation event

Represents a donor's recorded donation event.

### Collection unit

Represents the physical unit collected during the donation.

### Component unit

Represents a distinct component produced from a collection unit.

The relationship must support one donation event to a collection unit and, where processing produces multiple components, one collection unit to multiple component units.

Each unit and component must have a stable internal identifier and support the identifiers used by the authorised facility.

### QR and barcode requirements

- Scanning resolves an authorised unit record.
- Codes must not expose donor personal information.
- The server checks permissions before returning details.
- Identifier duplication is prevented.
- Scans must not independently authorise a state transition.
- Official identifiers remain associated with the internal identifier.

### Required unit history

For each unit, the platform must be able to reconstruct, where the relevant system participants have recorded the events:

- Originating donation event.
- Originating camp.
- Collection identifier.
- Handover and receipt.
- Processing and component derivation.
- Testing and release decisions.
- Storage movements.
- Reservations and issue.
- Transfers and returns.
- Expiry, discard or final recorded disposition.

The interface must explicitly show the last verified event and any gaps in external traceability.

## 7. Inventory model and consistency rules

### Authoritative inventory

Inventory must be derived from individual unit and component records, not independently maintained manual totals.

The application may maintain efficient current-state projections, provided they remain transactionally consistent with the authoritative records.

### Required distinctions

The system must distinguish:

- Physical units recorded.
- Units awaiting receipt reconciliation.
- Units awaiting processing.
- Units on hold.
- Released and available units.
- Reserved units.
- Issued units.
- Expired units.
- Discarded units.
- Units in transit or otherwise unavailable.

The exact states and transitions must be validated against the participating facility's approved procedures.

### Inventory invariants

1. A unit cannot be counted as available merely because it was collected.
2. A unit cannot be released by an unauthorised user.
3. An issued unit cannot simultaneously remain available.
4. A unit cannot be counted twice because of duplicate events.
5. Component creation must not double-count the parent collection as a separate transfusable component.
6. Stock changes must preserve an auditable explanation.
7. Concurrent operations must not create conflicting current states.
8. Expired, held, quarantined or otherwise ineligible units must not appear as available.
9. Reconciliation differences must create explicit exceptions.
10. Unknown or stale data must not be represented as confirmed zero or confirmed availability.

### Public availability

Public search should expose only the permitted summary:

- Facility.
- Blood group.
- Component.
- Reported available quantity.
- Source.
- Last-updated timestamp.
- Availability disclaimer.

It should not expose donor identities, internal unit identifiers or clinical records.

## 8. Core domain model

The following is a conceptual entity inventory, not the final Prisma schema.

| Domain | Proposed entities |
|---|---|
| Identity | User, Session, Role, OrganisationMembership |
| Organisation | BloodBank, OrganisationApplication, AuthorisationRecord |
| Donor | DonorProfile, ConsentRecord, DonorPreference |
| Camp | Camp, CampBloodBankAssociation, CampStaffMembership |
| Attendance | CampRegistration, CheckIn, ScreeningReference |
| Collection | DonationEvent, CollectionUnit |
| Processing | TestRecord, ProcessingEvent, ComponentUnit |
| Custody | Handover, HandoverItem, ReceiptRecord |
| Inventory | InventoryLocation, InventoryMovement, Reservation |
| Distribution | ReceivingFacility, IssueRecord, ReceiptAcknowledgement |
| Disposition | ReturnRecord, DiscardRecord, FinalDisposition |
| Patient coordination | BloodRequest, DonorInvitation, DonorResponse |
| Real-time | Notification, DomainEvent, OutboxEvent |
| Accountability | AuditEvent, ReconciliationCase |

Relationships, field definitions, deletion rules, unique constraints and indexes must be specified before implementation.

### History and current state

Critical changes must preserve historical events.

The platform may store a current-state representation for efficient queries, updated in the same database transaction as the corresponding event records.

History should not be silently rewritten to make current totals appear consistent. Corrections must be explicit, authorised and auditable.

## 9. API and service boundaries

The existing Express API should remain the application backend unless the architecture review identifies a concrete reason to change it.

Proposed service boundaries:

- Identity and Access Service.
- Organisation Service.
- Donor Service.
- Camp Management Service.
- Donation and Collection Service.
- Unit Traceability Service.
- Processing and Release Service.
- Inventory Service.
- Handover and Reconciliation Service.
- Patient Request and Matching Service.
- Notification and Event Delivery Service.
- Reporting and Audit Service.
- External Directory Integration Service.

These are logical service boundaries; they do not require separate microservices.

For the initial implementation, a modular monolith is the recommended default. It keeps transactions and deployment simpler while allowing clear domain separation.

Critical state changes must go through validated business services, not unrestricted direct updates from page components.

## 10. Real-time architecture

### Source of truth

PostgreSQL remains authoritative for persisted business state.

WebSockets provide live delivery to connected clients. They do not replace database transactions, authorisation, or audit records.

### Proposed event flow

1. A user submits an action through an authenticated API.
2. The backend checks permissions and business rules.
3. The backend commits the state change and required event records in a transaction.
4. A reliable event publisher delivers the committed event.
5. Relevant connected clients receive an authorised, minimal notification.
6. Clients update the affected interface.
7. Disconnected clients recover missed information from persisted state or notification history.

A transactional outbox is recommended for important events where losing a notification after a successful database commit would create operational risk.

### Initial event catalogue

| Event | Intended recipients |
|---|---|
| `DONOR_INVITATION_CREATED` | Invited donor |
| `DONOR_RESPONSE_RECORDED` | Requester and authorised participants |
| `BLOOD_REQUEST_CLOSED` | Relevant authorised participants |
| `CAMP_ASSOCIATION_UPDATED` | Relevant camp and bank users |
| `CAMP_SUBMISSION_CREATED` | Associated blood-bank staff |
| `HANDOVER_RECEIPT_RECORDED` | Relevant camp and bank users |
| `UNIT_STATUS_CHANGED` | Authorised inventory users |
| `INVENTORY_SUMMARY_UPDATED` | Authorised users and permitted public views |
| `DONATION_VERIFICATION_UPDATED` | Donor and authorised staff |
| `RECONCILIATION_EXCEPTION_CREATED` | Responsible operational staff |

These event names are proposed contracts. Payloads, versioning, permissions and delivery guarantees remain to be specified.

### Security requirements

- Authenticate WebSocket connections.
- Authorise every subscription and message.
- Scope events by user, organisation and purpose.
- Revalidate access when permissions change.
- Never broadcast sensitive information to global rooms.
- Do not trust client-supplied organisation IDs without server-side verification.
- Do not use socket delivery as proof that a critical action succeeded.

### Performance requirements

Before adding caching or infrastructure, measure the current bottlenecks.

Optimisation should include:

- Efficient Prisma queries.
- Appropriate database indexes.
- Pagination.
- Selective field retrieval.
- Avoiding N+1 queries.
- Reducing duplicate API requests.
- Targeted client-side updates.
- Safe caching for appropriate read-heavy data.
- Connection-pool and query monitoring.

Redis, background workers and additional infrastructure should be introduced when justified by the required reliability or measured load.

## 11. User experience requirements

### General principles

- Mobile-first design.
- Role-aware workspaces.
- Task-oriented navigation.
- Clear status and next-action indicators.
- Minimal unnecessary typing.
- Accessible controls and form labels.
- Responsive tables and search results.
- Clear loading, error, empty and success states.
- Confirmation for consequential actions.
- Helpful recovery from network interruptions.

### Navigation

The final navigation structure will be validated with user journeys rather than copied from a conventional sidebar.

Potential layouts include:

- Compact desktop top navigation.
- Contextual workspace navigation for staff.
- Bottom navigation for frequent mobile tasks.
- Prominent primary actions for camp collection workflows.
- Simplified patient and donor workspaces.

Each role should see only the navigation relevant to its tasks.

### Mobile camp operations

The camp interface should support:

- Fast donor lookup.
- Authorised donor registration.
- Attendance and screening records.
- Donation-event recording.
- Unit identifier entry or scanning.
- Record confirmation.
- Handover preparation.
- Recovery from temporary network failures.

Offline operation must be explicitly designed. Pending actions must not silently overwrite newer records or bypass critical validations.

### Accessibility

The platform should target recognised accessibility best practices, including keyboard operation, sufficient contrast, semantic structure, focus indicators and accessible form errors. Compliance must be assessed rather than assumed.

## 12. Privacy, security and audit

### Donor privacy

- Public search must not expose a donor's private identity or contact details without an authorised purpose.
- Donor search consent must be explicit and revocable.
- Camp staff receive only the information needed for their authorised tasks.
- Patient and donor contact sharing follows the established consent and acceptance rules.
- Recipient identity must not appear in the donor's public donation history.

### Clinical data

- Restrict access to authorised personnel.
- Avoid collecting information without a defined purpose.
- Keep clinical decision-making with qualified personnel.
- Record the identity and timestamp of authorised decisions.
- Define retention and correction policies.

### Audit events

For consequential operations, record:

- Actor.
- Organisation and role context.
- Action.
- Target record.
- Timestamp.
- Outcome.
- Reason or reference where required.
- Correlation ID.
- Relevant before/after state or a suitable immutable change reference.

Audit records must not expose passwords, session secrets or unnecessary sensitive information.

### Recovery and continuity

The design must cover:

- Database backups.
- Tested restoration.
- Access revocation.
- Notification recovery.
- Reconciliation after interruptions.
- Incident investigation.
- Data correction without silent history alteration.

## 13. Integration strategy

### eRaktKosh

Use the existing integration for public directory and externally reported availability where supported.

Preserve source attribution, freshness, error handling and the distinction between external summaries and participating-bank unit records.

### Participating blood banks

Begin with a controlled workflow that can be reconciled against the facility's existing records.

Before introducing automated integration, document:

- Current inventory software.
- Available identifiers.
- Data export or API capabilities.
- Update frequency.
- Ownership of each data field.
- Error and reconciliation procedures.

Avoid requiring duplicate data entry indefinitely when a reliable integration becomes possible.

### Receiving facilities

Design the issue and disposition interfaces now, but implement operational integrations when a participating receiving facility and validated workflow are available.

| Phase | Deliverable | Completion gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Discovery | Requirements and operational workflow | Workflow reviewed with relevant stakeholders |
| 1. Architecture | Data model, state machines, API contracts and migration plan | Design reviewed and invariants documented |
| 2. Identity and organisations | Verified onboarding, roles and permissions | Cross-organisation access tests pass |
| 3. Camps and donors | Camp approval, attendance and donation events | Test camp workflow reconciles |
| 4. Traceability | Collection and component unit identity | Unit history and duplicate prevention verified |
| 5. Handover | Manifest, receipt and reconciliation | Missing and discrepant records detected |
| 6. Inventory | Processing, release and unit-derived availability | Inventory reconciles with unit records |
| 7. Real-time | Authorised event delivery and recovery | Live updates and missed-event recovery verified |
| 8. Patient availability | Integrated stock search and donor fallback | Source and freshness represented correctly |
| 9. Final disposition | Receiving facility and downstream tracking | Defined end-to-end traceability demonstrated |
| 10. Pilot validation | Security, operational and usability validation | Participating organisations approve pilot readiness |

Each phase must have a test plan, documented decisions, known limitations and a completion review.

A phase is not complete simply because its pages load or its typecheck passes.

## 15. Acceptance criteria for the full system

The complete system should eventually demonstrate that:

1. Every participating organisation has a distinct verified identity and controlled access.
2. Every recorded collection unit has a unique identifier.
3. Derived components remain linked to their source collection.
4. Every consequential status change has an authorised, auditable basis.
5. Camp submissions can be reconciled against physical receipt.
6. Inventory quantities can be explained by underlying unit records.
7. Invalid or conflicting concurrent updates cannot corrupt inventory.
8. Expired, held, quarantined or issued units cannot incorrectly appear as available.
9. Public availability includes its source and freshness.
10. Donor and patient contact details are disclosed only to authorised recipients.
11. Live notifications are scoped correctly and can recover after disconnection.
12. The interface is usable on mobile devices by the intended users.
13. External facilities without integration have explicit traceability boundaries.
14. Backup, recovery, access revocation and audit procedures have been tested.
15. The participating organisations have validated the operational workflow before real-world use.
